Give an assistant useful context

How can an AI assistant remember what matters?

Work individually for 20 minutes. Use plain chat or the paper alternative. No coding, API key, paid account, or project feature is required. Never include private personal information.

1. Sort what should carry forward (3 minutes)

The outdoor club has a $25 event budget. Announcements must ask readers to RSVP. The venue and start time are undecided. You prefer unfamiliar terms explained in plain language. Today you want an announcement for the club's photo walk.

Which item belongs overall, which belong to this club project, and which belongs only in today's task? Explain one choice.

2. Write a short Markdown note (5 minutes)

Use a heading and a list. Include the club's facts and two observable instructions. Make clear what the assistant should do when information is missing. Keep today's request separate. You may draft on paper, then paste or type the note.

Check what the answer used

3. Predict, supply, inspect (6 minutes)

Predict two things you can check in the answer. In a fresh chat, paste your note with “Use these project instructions for this exercise.” Then ask: “Write the photo-walk announcement.” Record the actual answer and for each predicted rule, identify a line that follows or violates it (or record a missing line). Did it invent a venue or a start time?

Paper alternative: inspect this possible answer: “Join our photo walk at 3 PM in River Park! Bring your camera and meet other photographers.” Compare it with your note. Mark this as a paper prediction, not an actual chat test.

4. Revise and retry (4 minutes)

Make one rule easier to check. In another fresh chat, supply the revised note and the same task. Compare both answers. Do not assume a new chat has our note unless you supplied it or verified a configured mechanism. On paper, rewrite the possible answer to follow your revised rule and label it authored.

5. Choose a scope and explain the result (2 minutes)

Would you keep the club budget in overall instructions or project instructions? Why? Complete: “My note belonged at ___ scope; the answer showed ___.” Explain why an assistant saying “I remembered” is insufficient evidence.

Submit: your note, exact task, prediction, observed line (or labelled paper result), revised rule, and scope choice.
