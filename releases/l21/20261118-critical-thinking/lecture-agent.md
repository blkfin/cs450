## Treat an AI answer as evidence from an unreliable witness

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.unreliable_witness`
- Citations: `russell_sceptical`, `hw_faculty_critical`

- Russell: no grounds, no belief.
- A model works on probability, not experience.
- It can sound logical with no evidence behind it.

- **Hallucination**: An invented fact, stated as confidently as a real one.

## You are the detective, and the AI is the witness

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.detective_and_witness`
- Citations: `hw_faculty_critical`

| Role | In a case | In your project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Detective | Verifies the statement | You and your group |
| Witness | Confident, but prone to making things up | The AI tool |
| Court | Accepts only verified statements | Your group project |

Nothing goes into the artifact unverified.

## Five checks turn a witness statement into evidence

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.protocol_opener`
- Citations: `hw_faculty_critical`

- We know: the AI is a confident, unreliable witness.
- Not enough: your project needs claims you can stand behind.
- Evidence Protocol: five checks run before a claim is used.

| Step | Check |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Track the source (Chain of Custody) |
| 2 | Check the dates (Timeliness) |
| 3 | Corroborate the story |
| 4 | Scan for tampering (Omissions / Bias) |
| 5 | Verify the details (Precision) |

## Steps 1 to 3 check the source, the dates and a second source

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.steps_one_to_three`
- Citations: `hw_faculty_critical`

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Track the source | Click the links. Do they exist and support the claim? No source? Tag it: "Origin unknown: unreliable." |
| 2 Check the dates | Is the AI relying on older data to answer a question about today? Is this fresh evidence, or possibly no longer relevant? |
| 3 Corroborate the story | Find a second, non-AI source that confirms the key points. Verdict: matches, or contradicts? |

## Steps 4 and 5 look for gaps and verify one detail

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.steps_four_and_five`
- Citations: `hw_faculty_critical`

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 4 Scan for tampering | Are there suspicious gaps? What is not being said, and whose perspective is left out? Is the tone or language steering you? |
| 5 Verify the details | Pick one number, quote, or specific fact. Recompute it or find its original source and verify it exactly. |

- **Bias**: A slant from missing perspectives or steering language.

## Five logged checks become one ruling on the claim

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.verdict_opener`
- Citations: `hw_faculty_critical`

- We know: each member holds one step's evidence.
- Not enough: one check alone cannot clear a claim.
- The group pools all five and rules.

- **Admissible**: Verified enough to build on.
- **Inadmissible**: Flawed or false; it stays out as it stands.
- **Correction**: The specific change the claim needs before any use.

## Every group lands a verdict and a correction

- Source lineage: `lecture#sections.verdict_and_correction`
- Citations: `hw_faculty_critical`

- Inadmissible with no correction: not finished
- Admissible with no evidence logged: not finished

Pool your five steps on the worksheet, rule on the claim, and write the correction.
